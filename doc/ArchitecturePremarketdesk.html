PremarketDesk

There is no server, no queue, and no service. PremarketDesk is six Windows Task Scheduler jobs on one machine, each waking at its own time, reading files the earlier jobs left behind, and leaving files for the next. The schedule is the architecture.

01  Purpose

What this is for

PremarketDesk has two goals that live on different timescales, and most of the confusion about it comes from reading one and expecting the other.

The daily goal is orientation before the bell. At 08:53 an email arrives naming the stocks that gapped overnight, the headline behind each one, whether the premarket volume behind the move is unusual for that ticker or ordinary, and where price sits against the prior high, the premarket high, and the 200-day average. It replaces the hour most people spend scanning between 07:00 and 09:30. It is not a signal and cannot be one, because gap setups resolve in the first fifteen minutes and the report is read at 09:00.

The long goal is finding out whether the screen is worth anything. Every morning the system writes down what it claimed and where the levels were. Every night it writes down what actually happened. After enough sessions that record answers questions that cannot be answered today: whether gap plus catalyst plus premarket relative volume has an edge on this data rather than on the data of whoever published the thresholds, whether a high confluence score outperforms a low one, and which component of the score is carrying the result. Until then the thresholds in CRITERIA.md are seed values copied from a third party and the report is a well-formatted list of names that met a stranger's rules.

The briefing exists to keep the system in daily use long enough for the record to become worth reading. That is the whole design in one sentence.

Sample unit is the session, not the row. Twelve candidates from one morning share a market, a macro print, and often a sector move, so they are not twelve independent observations. Any analysis of the record aggregates to one figure per session before comparing conviction buckets.

02  Timeline

The weekday, end to end

Each job wakes on its clock time, does one thing, and exits. No job calls another. What passes between them is files on disk, which is why a dead job shows up downstream as a missing file with a stated reason rather than as a crash.

04  Morning flow

The morning relay

Each stage writes one artifact and the next stage reads it. Nothing talks to anything else at runtime. If a stage dies, its file is simply absent and the next stage records the absence in the packet's gaps_to_fill list rather than raising.

05  Opened up

Inside the scan

The scan does more work than anything else in the system and it is the only place where a decision is made. Eight ordered steps, no model involved in any of them.

06  Opened up

Inside the analyst pass

This is the boundary the whole design is built around. Everything to the left of it is deterministic and reproducible. Everything to the right is prose. The model never chooses a name, never sets a number, and never changes a conviction.

07  Coverage

The blind window and the night

Two facts force the night pipeline to exist. The premarket session opens at 04:00 but the collector only starts listening at 07:20, so the morning's premarket high honestly describes a partial window. And the vendor publishes its one-minute intraday bars several hours behind live, so the full truth does not exist anywhere until the evening.

A true high below the live high is suspicious, not impossible. The trades socket and the published bars can differ on odd lots, condition codes, and late corrections, so a legitimate feed difference will trip this. The backfill records the size of the discrepancy as a percentage rather than a boolean, so small feed noise stays visibly distinct from a real fault, and a human decides. Nothing is silently corrected.

What the socket costs on the vendor's counter, measured 2026-08-13. The old claim that the collector consumes no API calls was a client side observation about this process. It is now a vendor side measurement: measure_socket_cost.py read /api/user before and after collector-only runs while nothing else used the key. A 20 minute run with 10 connections, 10 full 38 symbol resubscriptions, and 9 reconnects moved the daily counter by exactly zero, and a second 20 minute run with 3 forced drops added (13 connections, 13 resubscriptions, 12 reconnects) also moved it by zero, so the per reconnect and per resubscribe cost is a measured zero, not an assumption. Reconnect and resubscription counts now land in each morning's packet next to bars_total, so a flaky morning is on the record. Scope: both runs rode the quiet evening tape; an earlier window that streamed 1,574 live trade messages straddled the counter's daily reset (20:00 ET, midnight UTC, also learned here), so the per message cost on a heavy live tape is the one number still owed. It is measurable any weekday by running measure_socket_cost.py inside the 04:00 to 07:15 window, before the scheduled jobs wake and while no other project touches the key.

What one bulk live snapshot costs, measured 2026-08-13 at 23:05 ET. measure_bulk_cost.py read /api/user, watched the meter sit still for a 45 second quiet window, made exactly one bulk live OHLCV request, and read the meter again. The counter moved by 100 for 18,341 returned symbol rows in one HTTP attempt. That reads as a flat per request rate, not a per symbol one, and the quiet watch saw zero drift from the script's own meter reads, so the whole 100 belongs to the bulk request. The design makes two of these calls each weekday, discover at 07:15 and scan at 08:45, about 200 counted calls a day against the shared 100,000: well under the 1,000 per call line at which the bulk calls would dominate the account, so the two call design stands. The same session recorded why the meter must be read rather than inferred: at 23:03 ET the counter already showed 49,999 used on a quota day that had opened at 20:00, none of it this project's spending.

08  Record

One picks row, three passes

The clearest way to see the system is to follow a single database row. The scan creates it in the morning, the backfill widens it the same night, and the outcomes pass returns one and five sessions later to record what the market actually did.

Every other failure either degrades the report or self-heals on the next run. That asymmetry is why the scan catches everything and raises nothing.

11  Rules

Load-bearing rules

Six decisions that shape everything above. Changing any of them changes the architecture rather than the configuration.

One vendor
EODHD is the only market data source

REST for quotes, history, news, and calendars. The trades websocket for live premarket. No second vendor, so there is never a question of which feed a number came from.

One place
Every threshold lives in CRITERIA.md

No Python file contains a screening number. The reader raises on anything missing, so a threshold cannot quietly exist in two places and drift.

The boundary
Python decides, the model narrates

Membership, eligibility, scores, and conviction are all computed before the packet is piped to the CLI. A containment check fails the run if the report names a ticker the packet does not carry.

No guessing
Missing evidence is null with a reason

A relative volume that cannot be computed honestly is null plus a reason string, never a substituted proxy. The disclaimer line names every such candidate by ticker.

No overwriting
Corrections sit beside the original

The night writes true premarket columns next to the morning's live values and never over them. The distance between them is a measurement the project needs, not an error to clean up.

Human gate
No email until a real morning is verified

While data/UNVERIFIED exists, sending is refused regardless of configuration. Only a person who has checked a live morning's numbers against a reference deletes it.
