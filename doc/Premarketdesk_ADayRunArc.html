PremarketDesk architecture

A Day in PremarketDesk

There is no server and no queue. The whole system is seven .bat files on one machine, registered as seven Windows Task Scheduler tasks, one per job, and a task carries every trigger its job has: the nightly fires at 22:15, again at 07:00, and on Sunday at 21:00 for the weekly universe rebuild, and the watchdog three times over, every 30 minutes from 07:25 to 09:25, again every 30 minutes from 12:25 to 13:25, and once more at 22:45, each .bat telling its firings apart by the clock. The seventh task is not a step at all but an instrument, the quota meter sampler, which takes one reading every thirty minutes of every day including weekends. Each task wakes at its clock time, reads the files earlier jobs left behind, and leaves files for the next. The architecture is the clock.

The day has a shape. The morning gathers evidence while it exists and publishes a judgement on it before the open. At noon a third pass asks what the session did with the morning's own picks and what else moved that the morning never named. The night comes back once the session is over, measures what was actually true, and books what one written rule would have done about it. The next morning reads that record back. This page follows that loop once, in order, and says what each stage of it is for. It describes the system as it stands and nothing else: what changed on which date is in CHANGELOG.md, why a choice that could have gone the other way went the way it did is in DECISIONS.md, and every threshold is in CRITERIA.md. Where a date appears below it is attached to a MEASUREMENT, because a measurement without the day it was taken is worth less than one with it.

The weekday, end to end

The jobs that build the day, one timeline

Every weekday runs itself. Discovery runs twice, at 03:55 and at 07:15, and reads no price from today at either hour, because no source on this plan has one for the whole universe before the open. It builds a candidate pool from four things that are knowable before the open (earnings landing between the prior close and this open, overnight news, prior session movers, and recent runners), ranks it by tier and then by measured gap propensity, and subscribes the collector to at most 42 names, with a floor of four slots per populated tier so a heavy earnings morning cannot spend the whole cap on one tier. The rest of the pool is written to watchlist.json marked not_subscribed, so the cut is auditable rather than invisible. The collector listens to live trades, the 08:45 chain turns evidence into an emailed report, the noon pass at 12:00 returns to those same picks once the session has opened, and the night jobs, shown in amber here and throughout this page, come back at 22:15 to write down what was really true. Sunday at 21:00 one more pass rebuilds the discovery universe for the coming week and measures the gap propensity the weekday pool is ordered by. The hour is chosen rather than convenient: the shared vendor quota rolls over at 20:00 ET, the counter does not roll punctually, and measured it lands 30 to 32 minutes late, so 21:00 clears the reset by roughly double the lag actually observed.

03:55 to 08:55

The morning pipeline: files passed hand to hand

Each stage writes one artifact and the next stage reads it. Nothing talks to anything else at runtime; if a stage dies, its file is simply missing and the next stage says so in the packet's gaps_to_fill instead of crashing. Every market number in every stage comes from EODHD, over REST or the trades websocket. One handoff is special: the collector is still appending to its bar file when scan reads it at 08:45, so scan freezes a snapshot copy at runs/<date>/premarket_snapshot.jsonl, parses only complete lines, and discards a partial trailing line, recording what it saw in the packet. And after deliver, a last step rebuilds site/PremarketDesk.html, the desk: one double click document carrying every session on file, on nine hash routes, one of which is the morning's report as written. It was build_archive's report archive until 2026-09-04, when the owner retired that page and the desk took its filename.

What the report itself holds is worth setting out, because three parts of it do not follow from the pipeline above. Every table has a plain English line under it saying what its columns mean, and the page ends with a short list explaining the words, because the report is read by people who do not trade and a row of Gap, RVOL, VWAP and Sigma is a wall to them. That wording lives in one file, core/glossary.py, so a word cannot come to mean two things in two places.

The day watchlist prints an Entry and a Stop column. Neither is advice and neither is new evidence: they are the two levels CRITERIA [Picks] already names, the premarket high and the premarket low, read through one function that the report and the database row both call, so the page a reader sees and the record the night books against cannot come to disagree. Technical signals is a table of levels for every candidate, and the write ups beneath it are for the names on a watchlist alone; each closes on a fixed opening, "What would say this is wrong:", and the rest of that line is written in words with the digits left out. It states the level and never restates its figure, because nothing in this system checks a number the model writes, and a line with no digits in it cannot invent one.

And a section called "Why these gapped" sits under the gappers table, one short paragraph a stock, written by a SECOND model call, morning/gap_reasons.py. It is deliberately separate from the narrative: it runs even when the narrative failed and the morning fell back to plain tables, because that morning needs the explanation most, and when it fails it costs that section alone. It only sees that stock's own headlines and has to quote the one it used; an answer citing a headline nobody supplied is discarded and the row says so. It stands in place of a Top headline column, which showed the NEWEST story the news feed had attached to a stock rather than the one that explains the move, and which is how a story about Palantir came to sit against MSTR.

Because that section is model written too, the guard that refuses sweeping claims about the candidate set reads the FINISHED report rather than just the narrative. See the architecture page for how the model's words are told apart from the project's own.

Under each headline the report quotes, one sentence says whether it is about the company itself, about its sector or the wider market, or about a peer, with one clause naming what the headline says happened. That is a judgement and it is asked for because three Python tests were built to make it mechanically and measured against the whole article corpus, and a wire piece headlined "Energy stocks lead in subdued final trading day of August" filed against a health care ticker beat two of them. It sits under the printed headline so a wrong call is visible, and nothing follows from it: the class, the score and the conviction keep the values Python gave them.

The Summary quotes three counted sentences about the list as a whole, which nothing in the report used to say: how the candidates fall by sector, how they fall by catalyst class, and which were also picked inside the last few recorded sessions. Nine of twelve in one sector is a fact about the morning rather than about any of the nine. They describe and never screen. And each candidate carries a line naming what evidence was missing for it, or no line at all when nothing was; a gap the whole measurable list shares is cut from the rows and said once in the disclaimer, so the one name with no baseline is not buried under eleven copies of a caveat they all carry.

One stage inside scan.py is worth separating out, because it does not behave like the rest. Everything above is a funnel: about 2,751 names in the universe, a few hundred gapping, twelve candidates, and of those twelve some number that clear the day or swing screen and reach the watchlist. That funnel answers one question, which is what might be worth trading. The notable movers section answers a different one: what MOVED, whether or not it is tradeable. It reads the whole universe rather than the candidate list, over four ranked lists of five, and it is explicitly not a watchlist. The section states that in seven fixed sentences it is not allowed to reword, and no row it produces can reach the picks table.

The reason it is separate is the reason it exists. A funnel that is working correctly still throws away every name that is unusual but untradeable, and on a morning where nothing clears the screens the report would otherwise say almost nothing while the market was busy. The cost is that a reader sees two kinds of list in one document, so every notable row carries the leg that produced it, the session it is as of, and whether the name also reached the watchlist, and a name can appear more than once, once per leg, because a row carries one window and one vintage.

12:00, once the session has opened

The noon pass: what the picks did, and what else moved

The morning publishes a judgement ninety minutes before the open and then has nothing further to say about the day it was about. Two of the questions it cannot answer are answerable by lunchtime, and a third weekday job asks them: what the morning's own picks did once the session opened, and what else moved that the morning never named. job_midday.bat runs midday.scan_midday and then midday.render_midday, and writes one packet and two renders into the same dated directory the morning used, runs/<date>/midday_packet.json, report_midday.md and report_midday.html. No table and no later step reads them; two renderers do. desk/compact.py inlines each day's report_midday.md beside that morning's report in site/PremarketDesk.html, and render_report.py links report_midday.html from the foot of the morning page once the 12:00 pass has written it. There is no email.

It grades against the levels the morning published, and the cost of that has to be said out loud. The carry through pass reads every live picks row for today and measures it against entry_ref and stop_ref, never against entry_ref_true and stop_ref_true, because those do not exist until the night's truth pass writes them at 22:15. The choice is between the morning's levels and no answer at all. The consequence is that the night ledger, booking the same trade against the corrected levels, will sometimes reach a different verdict. That is not a defect in either pass: it is two measurements of one question against two reference prices, and the noon one is built on the numbers a reader actually saw at 08:45. Nothing here is written to picks or to paper_trades. paper_trades is the night's, it is keyed on a rule version, and a second writer booking against uncorrected levels would destroy the comparison that table exists for.

never triggeredhigh below entry_ref. No position, and the session low is deliberately NOT read: a low with no trade under it stops nothing. CRITERIA writes this case down first because it is the easiest of the four to get wrong
gapped throughopen at or above entry_ref. The fill is the open, exactly as the night's rule fills a gap through, and because that fill is the session's FIRST print a low reaching stop_ref after it is unambiguously a stop out. Order is knowable here and only here
triggeredopen below entry_ref, high at or above it. The fill is entry_ref, and whether the session low came before or after that fill is UNKNOWABLE from a daily high and low, so a low at or under stop_ref is reported as the stop level reached with the sequence unknown, and never as a stop out
unknownany level the quote did not carry. Null with a reason, which is not a verdict and is never printed as one

The third row is the whole argument for moving [Collector] stop_time past the open, and the count of rows landing in it is printed on every edition rather than left to be noticed: a pass that is silent about how often it cannot answer reads exactly like a pass that always can. Minute bars carrying timestamps would turn that case into the second one's certainty, and the vendor's counter is not what stands in the way: the socket has been measured at zero cost per message across a heavy live tape. The other thing this pass cannot do is ask whether a level was transactable at all: fill_plausible is computed by the night out of Alpaca band volume and does not exist at noon, so a midday verdict on an illiquid level is arithmetic rather than a trade, and the report says so in its own words.

The second half ranks today's move across the whole universe, and it is a different question from the notable movers section at 08:45. That one ranks the previous COMPLETED session out of a file already on disk and costs nothing. This one asks what is moving mid session, and it costs one credit per universe name, which is most of the pass's measured 2,902 credits against a key that allows 100,000 a day and is shared with another project. The affordable design, pulling the news feed and quoting only the names carrying a headline, answers which names had news AND moved, which is not the question: a name that moved on no tagged headline would be invisible and the report would have no way of saying it was blind. So selection is on PRICE, news is fetched afterwards for the top of the list to explain what price has already found, and the quota preflight refuses rather than truncating, because half a universe is not a market wide scan and must not be published as one.

The denominator, which is the load bearing line of the whole pass

Every move reported here divides by the prior session's close, and that close comes from eod-bulk-last-day asked for by an EXPLICITLY NAMED date, one call for the whole exchange. It is emphatically not the quote's own previousClosePrice, which is the obvious field to reach for and the wrong one.

Measured on 2026-08-31 across the 2,391 universe names carrying both, previousClosePrice is not one quantity. It equalled the PRIOR session's close for about 34 percent of them and TODAY'S close for about 29 percent, with nothing in the payload saying which a given row holds, and SAIC.US matched neither. A further 359 names carried no previousClosePrice at all while carrying a previousCloseDate, and every one of those dates was correct. The field was RIGHT on rows whose price was wrong, so it is evidence about the vendor rather than a check on anything, and nothing branches on it.

It is the same shape as the defect vintage.py exists to catch: a report priced off the last completed session, from an endpoint whose name said live. A field's name is not its contract, and that holds just as well for a field called previous which is sometimes current.

No model runs at noon, and that is a decision rather than a saving. The morning calls the analyst because "what should I make of this" is an open question. Midday asks closed ones: a pick triggered or it did not, a name moved or it did not, and both are arithmetic that is already in the packet. So there is no narrative step, no containment check and no quantifier guard, because nothing here writes prose for them to police. The renderer builds its own markdown and escapes on the way out, since every headline in the movers section is third party text from a feed nobody here controls.

The hour is not arbitrary either. EODHD does not publish today's intraday bars until overnight, measured on 2026-08-31, so the endpoint the night measures the morning with is unavailable to this pass at any hour of the trading day. What is left is us-quote-delayed, whose REGULAR hours behaviour is the part that has been measured, on 2026-08-17 at a lastTradeTime of 09:56:12 against a 09:57 fetch, and whose premarket behaviour is untested. 12:00 sits well inside the window that was measured. The watchdog covers the job at 12:25, 12:55 and 13:25 for a reason worth saying: the weekday watchdog's last morning pass is 09:25 and the night one is at 22:45, so an unwatched midday failure would first be named by the next morning's packet, about eighteen hours later.

22:15, the part that happens after dark

Where the backfill happens, and why it exists at all

Two facts force the backfill to exist. First, the morning's premarket high, low, and VWAP describe a window that is still not the whole session. Until 2026-09-02 that was the large hole: the session opens at 04:00 ET and the collector only started listening at 07:20. The collector now opens with the session, and what is left is narrower but real. The window still ends at the 08:45 cutoff rather than at 09:30. A name that reaches the pool only at the 07:15 discovery pass is subscribed at the handover, so its tape begins at 07:20 and not at 04:00, which is what window_open_at and pm_window_starts_late record. And a name that never reached the pool at all has no tape of any kind. Second, EODHD publishes its one minute intraday bars a few hours behind live, so the full 04:00 to 09:30 truth simply does not exist anywhere until the evening.

So the day's numbers are written twice. At 08:45 the scan records what the collector actually heard, labelled partial where it was partial. At 22:15 the backfill fetches the now published intraday bars and writes pm_high_true, pm_low_true, and pm_vwap_true into the same picks rows, next to the morning values, never over them. The gap between the two column families is itself a measurement: it is exactly what listening to part of a session rather than all of it costs, and it is the record that priced the move to 04:00.

That last sentence needs one qualification, and it is the important one. The gap between the two column families conflates THREE causes and cannot separate them: the collector's late start, a feed disagreement inside the minutes both sources saw, and the window END, since the true window ran to 09:30 while the morning's stops at the 08:45 cutoff. The pm_*_collector_window columns exist to pull them apart. Full against collector window is what the window bounds cost; collector window against the live value is the feed disagreement on identical minutes, and only the second is a statement about the collector.

The same night then takes the measurement again from a second vendor, Alpaca's full SIP tape, which is a complete one. That pass writes pm_volume_true, and it writes entry_ref_true and stop_ref_true: the reference levels every excursion is measured from, taken off the whole tape rather than off a socket sample. Then it asks whether those levels were prices anyone could have transacted at. That chain is the next section.

Where, concretely, does it run?

  1. Task Scheduler fires PremarketDesk\nightly at 22:15 on weekdays.
  2. That runs tasks\job_nightly.bat, which first asks ops.market_today whether the market traded at all and stands the whole night down on exit 3. That guard is the first of the twelve steps and the one most easily read past, because it is the only one whose success looks like nothing happening. Then it refreshes the exchange calendar so the 08:45 window never has to, then runs night.backup_evidence first of all, because it copies the day's premarket capture, its two sidecars, the packet and the report in both markdown and HTML outside the working tree, and those six are the only artifacts here that cannot be rebuilt: a report least of all, because the same input does not produce the same words twice, then python -m night.backfill_premarket, then night.fill_outcomes, then night.true_volume, then night.paper_ledger, then night.pool_recall, then night.prune_data, then night.weekly_page, then desk.render on this machine, with desk.compact between recall and the prune. The ledger comes after truth and not before it, because it reads entry_ref_true, stop_ref_true and fill_plausible and every one of those is written by that step. Every job runs by module name rather than by file path. Only two steps can stop the chain, the backfill and the outcome fill; the calendar refresh, the backup, truth, the paper ledger, recall, the compaction, the prune, the weekly page and the desk each record their exit code and never fail it, but NOT all for one reason, and the single reason this sentence used to give was false for two of them. Most of these are recorded and never fail because nothing downstream reads what they write, so a bad measurement or an unmade copy must not cost the desk its rebuild. Truth and the compaction are read downstream and never fail anyway, because what depends on them DEGRADES rather than breaks: the ledger books nothing at all when truth left no _true levels, and the prune's third interlock simply declines to drop a session's duplicate snapshot until that session's desk.json.gz exists, so a missed compaction costs disk and never data. [corrected 2026-09-04: was three steps and named the archive, which could stop both chains; the desk replaced it that morning and deliberately cannot, because a report that was delivered is not undone by a page that did not draw.]
  3. Backfill writes the true premarket columns into the picks table of data\premarketdesk.db, and while the intraday feed is open it also runs the definitive collector volume check, saved to runs\<date>\verify_intraday.json. The true premarket columns are gated on the day having live picks rows, because that is what they widen. The volume check is not, and the distinction carries real weight: sitting behind the early return for a day with no picks would silence the instrument on exactly the nights the question is most urgent. So it runs first and reads the collector bar file rather than the table, and a separate sweep picks up any recent session that was collected and never measured.
  4. Everything it prints lands in logs\nightly-<date>.log.

22:15, the second half of the night

From a level, to a fill, to a booked trade

The backfill above answers what the premarket session did. It does not answer the question a reader actually has, which is whether any of it was worth anything. Four passes run in order to get there, and each one exists because the one before it opened a question it could not close. None of them touches the morning path or the screen.

The chain is: a level, then whether that level was real, then what a rule would have done with it, then whether the score saw any of it coming. Every step writes beside what came before it and never over it.

1. True reference levels

night/true_volume.py

  • entry_ref is pm_high, a socket SAMPLE
  • a sample understates a maximum
  • and overstates a minimum
  • so every excursion is biased
  • entry_ref_true is the same level off the whole tape

2. Fill plausibility

night/true_volume.py

  • a level is not a price you could get
  • volume within 0.5% of it, in dollars
  • three states, never a boolean
  • plausible / implausible / unknown

3. The paper ledger

night/paper_ledger.py

  • rules written in CRITERIA first
  • v1 and v2 booked side by side
  • books against the measured levels
  • skips an implausible fill, with the reason
  • paper_trades, keyed on rule_version

4. The score watch

night/weekly_page.py

  • the score ordered outcomes backwards
  • grouped by bucket and by component
  • both denominators, always
  • pre-registered, judged later

The one that matters most: the outcome columns describe the wrong day

The columns that say what happened next describe the session AFTER the one the report was about. That off by one is real, it is known, and it is left in place on purpose.

The scan runs at 08:45 on the pick's own date and the report is about the open ninety minutes later. But next_day_open, next_day_high, next_day_low, next_day_close, pm_high_broke_next_day, mfe_pct, mae_pct and the two new _true excursions all describe the session AFTER that one. The session the report was actually about is measured by none of them.

AXTI, picked 2026-08-27, entry_ref 70.94
its own session: opened 70.30, reached 70.85 → a miss by 0.13%
next_day_high: 65.4155, from 2026-08-28 → mfe_pct reads -7.79

Nothing in the outcome columns is repointed. Doing so would rewrite the meaning of every row already in the table and destroy the comparison. The ledger fetches its own bars for its own session instead, and the mismatch is written into CRITERIA [Paper], into paper_ledger's docstring, and into the ledger's own summary line, which states on every printing that the bound beside the booked P&L comes from a different day.

Two of these passes decide what the record says, not just what it holds

Measuring the reference levels moved both excursion medians across zero. On the sampled levels the median name looked to have run +0.81 percent past its entry reference and given back -1.92 against its stop. On the measured ones it is -2.13 and +0.15, and the count that reached the entry reference at all falls from 29 of 48 to 20. On the sampled levels alone the record would report a median name as having reached a level it never reached. That is the whole argument for the pass: not that the sampled numbers are imprecise, but that they have the sign wrong.

The obvious explanation for that gap is the wrong one, and saying so is the more useful result. The gap between the live and measured levels looks like the socket missing prints inside the minutes it heard. Split into its two causes it is +0.10 percent at the median for the sampling and +0.98 for the 04:00 to 07:20 stretch nobody was listening to. Ten to one the other way. The socket reproduces the extremes of the minutes it does hear almost exactly, so the fix belonged at [Collector] start_time and not at the feed. That is the measurement the 04:00 start was made on, on 2026-09-02, and the same split is what will say afterwards whether it worked.

Fill plausibility is three states because a fourth would be a lie. plausible, implausible, unknown. A row with no evidence behind it is not a row that failed, and no row is called plausible on absent evidence. A session the morning recorded no window for is MARKED unknown rather than left null, because a null verdict is a fourth state the column does not have and would read downstream as a row nobody asked about.

The rule, in full, because a rule that lives in code is one nobody can argue with

universeevery live picks row. Not only the eligible ones: day_eligible and swing_eligible travel onto the ledger row as GROUPING columns instead, so a later pass can ask whether the screen's verdict separated outcomes at all
skipany row whose fill_plausible is not 'plausible'. Written with its reason, never dropped
triggerthe first minute of the regular session whose high reaches entry_ref_true
entry pricemax(entry_ref_true, that minute's open). A session that gaps straight through the resting order fills at the open, which is the common case for a gap screen. HUT on 2026-08-27 opened 89.46 against an 88.90 trigger; booking the level would have turned a 3.00 percent loss into 2.38
stopstop_ref_true, the measured premarket low
same minutea minute that both triggers and reaches the stop is booked STOPPED. One bar carries no sequence, so the losing reading is taken
exitthe close of the session, if the stop was never reached. No target, deliberately: a target makes this a family with a parameter to fit, and holding to the close has nothing to tune
never firedno trade, and a NULL P&L. Zero would read as a flat trade, and 28 of the first 66 never fired
size10,000 dollars, whole shares, floor divided. Fixed notional because this table holds prices from 5.64 to 1,585

Two other definitions of fill plausibility were tried and refused, both caught by the calibration rather than by review, and both written up in DECISIONS.md. Counting a minute by its own volume weighted price is the obvious rule and measures the wrong thing: entry_ref is a session HIGH, an extreme no whole minute averages near, so BABA's 2,986,339 premarket shares over 268 minutes scored a band volume of zero. It ranked the most liquid names in the table as the least fillable. Requiring a minute COUNT as well fails the other way: MSTR traded 49,768 shares inside the band in a single minute, 1.4 million dollars, and calling that a print because it lasted one bar measures duration rather than liquidity. The verdict rests on notional alone and the minute count is recorded because it says how loose the volume bound is.

The band volume is an upper bound and is called one everywhere. A one minute bar carries o, h, l, c and v and no distribution, so a minute running from below up into the band counts whole. Correcting it needs trade level data this plan does not buy, so it is stated as a bound, the way premarket RVOL already is.

The fill band floor is derived rather than asserted. [Truth] min_fill_band_notional is 250,000, and that is not a round number somebody liked: [Paper] names a 10,000 dollar position at a 4 percent participation cap, and 10,000 / 0.04 is exactly 250,000. One shipped value, in [Truth], with the coupling machine checked, the same shape as the analyst timeout and the watchdog's stale window.

What the ledger is for

Why there is one rule, and why the second version changes only the size

A ledger that loses money looks like a verdict on the screen. It is not, and separating those two things is most of what this stage achieves. A rule has a trigger, a stop, an exit and a size, and a losing rule can fail at any one of them while the other three are sound. Booking the trade makes each of them a separate, answerable question.

The first thing it separated: the trigger from the exit

median best price reached while held +1.84% median given back by the hold-to-close -3.91 points losers that had been up more than 1% 6 of 11 losers never up more than 0.5% 2 of 11

Every trade the rule took was in profit at some point while it was open. NSSC was up 10.81 percent and closed -18.22. WOLF was up 6.36 and closed -7.72. PLAB was up 1.15 and closed -19.18. A trigger that produced random entries could not do that, so the loss is not evidence against the trigger and the screen behind it. It is evidence about what happens after the entry.

But in units of the risk taken it is weak, and that is the finding. The median trade reached 0.46 of the risk it was carrying and only 4 of 16 ever reached 1R. A trade that risks 5.89 percent to reach 0.46 of that cannot work at any hit rate, and the two largest losses were the two widest stops.

The stop was never designed to be a stop. CRITERIA [Picks] says of entry_ref and stop_ref: "references for outcome measurement in later nightly jobs, never advice". The premarket low was chosen because it is a traded extreme that keeps excursion math interpretable. v1 borrowed it as a trade level because it was the level that existed, and on a gapper the premarket range is routinely 20 percent.

The second version changes exactly one thing, on purpose

The position size, from a fixed notional to a fixed dollar risk. Entry, stop, exit, tie break and skip rule are byte for byte v1's, so the two book the same trades and any difference is attributable to the sizing alone.

Two other candidates were refused and the refusals are the useful part. An exit rule, a target or a trail, would capture some of that 3.91 point give-back and is a free parameter that 16 trades will fit any value of. A different stop reference changes which trades exist, so the versions would stop booking the same trades and nothing could be attributed.

The risk budget holds TOTAL risk constant. v1's 16 trades carried 12,354 dollars of risk between them, a mean of 772; risk_notional is 750. The versions put the same money at risk and differ only in how it is spread. A smaller number would have manufactured a better v2.

So the two versions book the same sixteen trades and every per-trade percent return is identical under both. That is not a disappointing result, it is the control working: sizing cannot change what a trade did, only how much of the account was standing behind it. What differs is the shape of the loss, and the widest-stop trades stop dominating it. CRITERIA [Paper] pre-registers the verdict at 200 trades across 60 sessions, written down before the count gets there so the number cannot be chosen after the fact.

What one row looks like when the night is done

One picks row, filled in four passes, and a ledger row beside it

The clearest way to see the flow is to follow a single database row. The scan creates it in the morning, the backfill and the truth pass widen it the same night, and the outcomes pass returns one and five trading sessions later to record what the market did next. Those outcome rows are the project's long game: the CRITERIA.md thresholds are unvalidated seed values until enough filled rows say otherwise. And the sample unit for that judgment is the session, not the row: twelve candidates from one morning share the same market, the same macro print, and often the same sector move, so they are one observation, not twelve. Any later analysis aggregates to one figure per session before comparing conviction buckets, and both fill_outcomes.py and weekly_page.py print both counts to keep that distinction visible.

A fifth pass writes to a different table. paper_ledger.py applies the one written rule to each pick and books the result into paper_trades, one row per pick per rule version. That is what makes mfe_pct a diagnostic rather than a result: the excursion says how far the tape ran past a reference at its best moment, which a real rule captures only with perfect exit timing and usually does not capture at all.

08:45, what the night taught the morning

The fill warning, which is a warning and never an approval

The night's fill verdict is the one thing it measures that the morning can act on. A third of what the report publishes has a headline level no market stands behind, and a reader who finds that out three weeks later in a database is not helped by it.

The morning cannot run the real check, because Alpaca refuses a session that is still running. So it computes its own band from the collector's sample at 08:45 and prints it in Skips and traps. Being a worse instrument than the night's is the design constraint, and the asymmetry is how it is handled: it is allowed to say a level looks thin and it is never allowed to say one looks fine.

morning: thincaught 6 of the 10 the night calls untradeable, and flagged 6 of the 44 that were fine
morning: not flagged38 the night agreed with, and 4 it did not. Four in ten untradeable levels get past this test, which is why its silence means nothing
morning: unknown12, the session with no collector coverage. Never "not flagged", which a reader would take for a clean bill

It cannot be calibrated better and the reason is structural: the morning centres its band on pm_high and the night on entry_ref_true, which differ by a median 1.19 percent and by up to 20.9, so on the names that matter most the two bands do not overlap. The socket's share of the band ran 0.017 to 1.158 of the night's figure across the same rows. A floor sweep from 5,000 to 100,000 dollars trades one error for the other at every step and never removes either.

It keeps its own vocabulary, thin and not flagged, never the night's plausible: a reader seeing that word at 08:45 would take it for the verdict that cannot exist until the session is over. It reads its band width from [Truth] rather than restating it, so the two bands cannot drift apart. And REPORT_TEMPLATE.md tells the model, in the section itself, never to write that a level is tradeable.

08:50, where the loop closes

What the record is worth to a reader, and what it is not

Everything above this line is apparatus. It measures the reference levels, it measures whether they were fillable, it books a rule against them, and it watches the score. On its own none of it changes what somebody does at 08:50 with ten tickers in front of them, and a page about the arc of a day has to be honest that the apparatus is not the point of the day.

The obvious way to close that gap is the wrong one. A list of last week's winners and losers is worth nothing to somebody reading this morning: PLAB losing 19 percent last Wednesday says nothing about today. No amount of measurement will make an individual past outcome predictive, and a report that implied otherwise would be worse than one that stayed quiet.

What carries something is the SHAPE of what those trades did, which is a different quantity. Not which names worked, but how long a name that worked took to work. The ledger records the two timings that carry it, and the morning reads them back.

The two timings answer different questions, which is why there are two

minutes_to_trigger runs from the OPEN and answers whether a name is still worth watching at 10:00. minutes_to_peak runs from the ENTRY and answers whether the one you are in is done. Measured from the open the second would fold the wait into the hold, and a name that triggered at 09:31 would be indistinguishable from one that triggered at 14:20, which is precisely the distinction the numbers below rest on.

mfe_pct_held is what the POSITION was worth while it was open. It is not picks.mfe_pct_true, which is a bound over the whole of the FOLLOWING session measured from a reference level rather than from a fill. Two different quantities that would read as the same thing if they shared a name.

What the shape looks like, on the record as it stood at sixteen trades

triggered within 30 minutes of the open14 of 16, at a median of 1 minute
triggered later than that2, at 291 and 337 minutes, making +0.60 and +0.36 percent
never triggered at all28 picks, median -1.97 percent open to close, and 7 of the 28 finished above their open
peaked within 10 minutes of entry10 trades, of which 10 closed BELOW entry
peaked more than 100 minutes after entry4 trades, of which 4 closed ABOVE it

The last two rows are the ones worth the whole stage. A name that makes its high in the first ten minutes and fades did not recover once in ten tries; the four that worked were still making highs an hour or more in. That is a statement about shape, it survives not knowing a single ticker, and it is the one thing the record can hand a reader before the open.

Ten and four are not sample sizes, and the template enforces that they are not written as though they were. Written as "cut the ones that stall and let the winners run" this becomes a strategy fitted to eight sessions and stated with the authority of a generated report, which is worse than saying nothing at all. So the report section quotes every count with its denominator, names the rule version that produced it, says the ledger is as of LAST NIGHT because tonight's pass has not run and today's picks are in no figure, and is FORBIDDEN from turning any count into an instruction. Three specimens of the phrasing it must not use are written into REPORT_TEMPLATE.md, and so is a ban on the words pattern, signal, edge and tendency: counts over a record this small support none of them.

And the morning pays nothing for it. The record block is one read of a local table, no vendor call and no new evidence, which is what lets it sit inside a window that has forty five minutes and one chance. paper_ledger reaches its Alpaca client inside book() rather than at module scope, so the scan can import the ledger without pulling an HTTP client into the 08:45 window at all.

minutes_to_triggerbars from the session open to the first minute whose high reached entry_ref_true. NULL, never zero, on a trade that never fired: zero would say it triggered instantly, which is a trade that happened
minutes_to_peakbars from the ENTRY to the highest high while the position was open
mfe_pct_heldthe best price reached while held, as a percent of the fill
record_so_far()every count over BOTH denominators, rows and sessions, because twelve names from one morning share a tape and are one observation. A pick skipped on evidence is counted separately from one that never triggered: the first is a fact about the level, the second about the session

Degradation, job by job

When something fails

The chain stops at the first failure, evidence gaps degrade to nulls with written reasons, and staleness is refused loudly in two places. Any job reading universe.json exits and names the file's age once it passes ten days. And scan.py calls vintage.py after pricing and before scoring, so a packet whose price timestamps, prior close dating, or high against close consistency say it is not this morning's data is refused outright. That second gate has no degrade path, because a stale price is not thin evidence a report can hedge around, it is a wrong number wearing the costume of a right one. vintage.py rewrites data/UNVERIFIED with every failing row named, nothing is scored, no packet is written, and scan exits non-zero, which stops the chain before the analyst. While the data/UNVERIFIED gate marker exists, no email goes out in any of these rows regardless of what else succeeded.

a market holiday arrives Every weekday job runs market_today.py first. On a weekend or an official exchange holiday (cached from EODHD exchange-details, refreshed by the nightly so the 08:45 window never fetches it) the job logs one line and exits cleanly: no watchlist from stale quotes, no empty collection, no report about a session that does not exist. If the calendar is unreachable and no cache exists, the guard assumes the market is open, because a false closed silently loses a real morning. That trade is only safe while "no cache" is rare, so the refresh overwrites only once the vendor has answered. Deleting the cache and then fetching would let one vendor outage at 22:15 leave no holiday list at all, and the next morning would run on the assumption that the market was open.
universe.py fails Sunday The week runs on the last good universe.json until it turns ten days old, then discover and scan refuse to run, printing the age and the limit. No packet, no report, no email past that point.
discover.py fails at 03:55 There is no watchlist when the collector starts, so it waits for today's file, checking on the handover's own cadence, and refuses only if nothing has arrived by 07:20. No monitor pass runs between 04:00 and 07:25, so a failed 03:55 pass costs the provisional tape until the 07:15 pass lands or a hand rerun writes the file, whichever is first. The watchdog reruns discover whenever a rewrite is free, which it is while no subscription list exists and, from then on, while the collector is still rereading the watchlist, until its 09:25 stop; it restarts a collector that is not alive inside the window. If the 07:15 pass succeeds, the day runs as it did before this schedule existed: a collector window that opens at the handover instead of at the session open, recorded as such in window_open_at rather than presented as a full window.
discover.py fails at 07:15 The collector keeps the 03:55 pool. Every refusal inside the handover returns stay put, because the tape already being written is real and a premarket cannot be bought back later: a missing file, an unreadable one, one stamped another day, an empty pool, or any exception at all leaves the run on the pool it opened with and records why. What is lost is the names that only became discoverable after 03:55, mostly the overnight news filed in between, and the warmed volume baselines. Scan does not rebuild membership and makes no bulk call of its own: it reads the subscribed rows of watchlist.json, so a missing watchlist leaves it with no candidates at all and gaps_to_fill says exactly that. A watchlist that is present and subscribes nobody says so separately, because an empty pool and an absent file are different failures. A watchlist that is not today's is refused outright at both the start and the handover, with no override outside the monitor's last resort branch, so the morning never runs on another session's pool wearing the costume of this one. Either way the report goes out with the holes named in the disclaimer. That last sentence is only true while the code actually takes the degrade path, which is why both routes into it are claims in the suite: every per candidate stage sits inside one branch of scan.py, and an empty pool reaching code outside that branch would end the scan with an exception instead of a zero candidate packet, stopping the chain and costing the whole morning.
collector never connects No minute bars, and price comes from the collector and nowhere else, so no candidate has a premarket price. Every subscribed name leaves the candidate list for the packet's dropped_no_coverage with its reason instead of being published at a stale price, so the packet carries zero candidates and gaps_to_fill names the missing collector file and every dropped name. The report still goes out: the market snapshot falls back to the end of day feed, each row labelled source eod and prior_session_only, the candidate tables are empty, and the disclaimer carries the reason.
scan.py fails at 08:45 No packet, so the chain stops before the model. No report, no email; the morning log ends at the scan step's exit code.
analyst.py fails or times out Two attempts at 1007 seconds each, which is three times the slowest morning on record, 335.7 seconds, so two exhausted attempts end at 09:18:53, eleven minutes before the open. The rule is fixed and the evidence under it moves, so the cap is re-derived rather than set once, and [Monitor] job_log_stale_after_s is derived from it in turn, at 2200 seconds, because a healthy analyst step the watchdog reads as a dead job gets relaunched mid run. Then the deterministic fallback: report.md is rebuilt straight from packet.json with the slots filled by fixed sentences, the disclaimer carries the failure reason, analyst_usage.json records status timeout, failed, quantifier or slots. The last is the slots mode's own refusal: an answer that altered the report outside its slots or left one unfilled on both attempts, kept beside the report as report.slots-rejected-N.md. Render and deliver carry on, the email still goes.
deliver.py fails The report is on disk in runs/<date>/ and the log holds Resend's answer. No email that morning and no morning archive rebuild, but the evening rebuild still catches the day; nothing else retries until the next scheduled morning.
desk/render.py fails site/PremarketDesk.html keeps its previous build. Every other artifact of the day is already on disk, and the next rebuild, evening or morning, replaces the file whole because every build is a full rebuild.
the midday pass fails at 12:00 Nothing downstream notices, because nothing downstream reads it: the midday packet and its two renders are read by no other job, and no email is waiting on them. The watchdog returns at 12:25, 12:55 and 13:25, and it REPORTS the failure without ever rerunning the job, which makes midday the one exception among the five jobs ops/monitor_jobs.py watches. Two reasons point the same way. The 12:00 sweep spends about 2,902 credits on a key shared with another project, and job_midday.bat sets PMD_JOB, so a relaunch resolves through core/artifacts.py as the owner of today and REPLACES the 12:00 packet with a later measurement. The worst case is exactly the one most likely to bring the watchdog here: a scan that wrote its packet and a render that failed, where a rerun spends the whole sweep again to redo a step that makes no vendor call, and overwrites the good half on the way. A human runs tasks/job_midday.bat if the report is still wanted.
nightly jobs fail The true premarket columns and outcomes stay null. Both jobs are idempotent and fill only what is missing, so the next night's run picks up the backlog on its own. The morning report does not ignore it, and that is deliberate: every scheduled step records its outcome to data/job-status.jsonl as it exits, and the next morning's disclaimer names any step that has not succeeded inside the window CRITERIA.md gives it. A nightly that fails quietly is exactly the failure nothing else in the system would notice.

Load bearing rules

Six decisions that shape everything above

EODHD is the only vendor a published number comes from.
REST for quotes, history, news, calendars; the websocket for live trades. Nothing in selection, collect, morning or midday calls a second vendor, so there is never a question of which feed a number in the report came from. The night is the deliberate exception and it is named rather than hidden: true_volume and paper_ledger both read Alpaca, because measuring the record against a second, complete tape is the entire point of those passes. What keeps the boundary is structural rather than a promise. Alpaca's free plan serves the sip feed for a session that is OVER and refuses a running one with 403, so no version of this reaches 08:45, and every column it writes carries truth_source, because a _true suffix is not a provenance.
Every threshold lives in doc/CRITERIA.md.
No Python file contains a screening number. criteria.py is a strict reader that raises on anything missing, so a threshold cannot quietly exist in two places.
Python decides, the model narrates.
Membership, eligibility, scores, and conviction are computed deterministically, and since 2026-09-02 so is the report itself: analyst.py writes every table, count and quoted sentence from the packet and the claude CLI fills the marked prose slots. A containment check fails the run if that prose mentions a ticker the packet does not carry.
Missing evidence is null, with a written reason.
A premarket RVOL that cannot be computed honestly is null plus a reason string, never a substituted guess. The report's disclaimer line names every such candidate.
The paper rule is written in CRITERIA before it is written in code.
Entry, stop, exit, size and every refusal live in CRITERIA [Paper] and paper_ledger.py applies them. One rule, not a family: no target, because a target is a free parameter that sixteen trades will fit any value of, and a rule with something to tune is a rule that cannot be wrong. Versions are booked side by side over the same trades, each differing from the last in exactly one thing, so a change that helped can be told from a change that merely happened.
Email is gated until a human verifies a real morning.
While data/UNVERIFIED exists, deliver.py refuses to send no matter what keys are configured. Only a human deletes that file.
