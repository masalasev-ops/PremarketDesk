PremarketDesk architecture

A Day in PremarketDesk

There is no server and no queue. The whole system is seven .bat files on one machine, registered as nine Windows Task Scheduler tasks: two of them are registered twice, the nightly at 22:15 and again at 07:00, and the watchdog every 30 minutes from 07:25 to 09:25 and once more at 22:45. The ninth is not a step at all but an instrument, the quota meter sampler, which takes one reading every thirty minutes of every day including weekends. Each task wakes at its clock time, reads the files earlier jobs left behind, and leaves files for the next. The architecture is the clock. One further .bat file sits in tasks\ and is a research probe rather than pipeline: job_probe_socket_cap.bat, which is not in register_tasks.ps1's schedule and is armed one morning at a time with register_tasks.ps1 -Probe YYYY-MM-DD. It is armed for 2026-08-21 06:30. job_probe_alpaca_live.bat and job_probe_live_v1.bat sat beside it until 2026-08-20 and were deleted once both questions were answered and recorded in DECISIONS.md.

The weekday, end to end

The jobs that build the day, one timeline

Every weekday runs itself. Discovery reads no price from today, because at 07:15 no source on this plan has one for the whole universe. It builds a candidate pool from four things that are knowable before the open (earnings before today's open, overnight news, prior session movers, and recent runners), ranks it by tier and then by measured gap propensity, and subscribes the collector to at most 42 names, with a floor of four slots per populated tier so a heavy earnings morning cannot spend the whole cap on one tier. The rest of the pool is written to watchlist.json marked not_subscribed, so the cut is auditable rather than invisible. The collector listens to live trades, the 08:45 chain turns evidence into an emailed report, and the night jobs, shown in amber here and throughout this page, come back at 22:15 to write down what was really true. Sunday at 21:00 a sixth pass rebuilds the discovery universe for the coming week and measures the gap propensity the weekday pool is ordered by. It ran at 20:00 until 2026-08-16, which was the exact instant of the quota reset, and at 20:30 until 2026-08-17, which assumed the vendor's counter rolls on the hour; it does not, and the observed roll was 30 to 32 minutes late.

07:15 to 08:55

The morning pipeline: files passed hand to hand

Each stage writes one artifact and the next stage reads it. Nothing talks to anything else at runtime; if a stage dies, its file is simply missing and the next stage says so in the packet's gaps_to_fill instead of crashing. Every market number in every stage comes from EODHD, over REST or the trades websocket. One handoff is special: the collector is still appending to its bar file when scan reads it at 08:45, so scan freezes a snapshot copy at runs/<date>/premarket_snapshot.jsonl, parses only complete lines, and discards a partial trailing line, recording what it saw in the packet. And after deliver, a last step rebuilds site/PremarketDesk.html, the double click archive of every morning's report.

One stage inside scan.py is worth separating out, because it is the newest and it does not behave like the rest. Everything above is a funnel: 2,754 names in the universe, a few hundred gapping, twelve candidates, and of those twelve some number that clear the day or swing screen and reach the watchlist. That funnel answers one question, which is what might be worth trading. Layer 4, the notable movers section added on 2026-08-20, answers a different one: what MOVED, whether or not it is tradeable. It reads the whole universe rather than the candidate list, over four ranked lists of five, and it is explicitly not a watchlist. The section states that in seven fixed sentences it is not allowed to reword, and no row it produces can reach the picks table.

The reason it is separate is the reason it exists. A funnel that is working correctly still throws away every name that is unusual but untradeable, and on a morning where nothing clears the screens the report would otherwise say almost nothing while the market was busy. The cost is that a reader now sees two kinds of list in one document, so every notable row carries the leg that produced it, the session it is as of, and whether the name also reached the watchlist, and a name can appear more than once, once per leg, because a row carries one window and one vintage.

22:15, the part that happens after dark

Where the backfill happens, and why it exists at all

Two facts force the backfill to exist. First, the premarket session opens at 04:00 ET but the collector only starts listening at 07:20, so the morning's premarket high, low, and VWAP honestly describe a partial window. Second, EODHD publishes its one minute intraday bars a few hours behind live, so the full 04:00 to 09:30 truth simply does not exist anywhere until the evening.

So the day's numbers are written twice. At 08:45 the scan records what the collector actually heard, labelled partial where it was partial. At 22:15 the backfill fetches the now published intraday bars and writes pm_high_true, pm_low_true, and pm_vwap_true into the same picks rows, next to the morning values, never over them. The gap between the two column families is itself a measurement: it is exactly what starting the collector at 07:20 costs.

That last sentence was wrong for a year of this project's short life and was corrected on 2026-08-28. The gap between the two column families conflates THREE causes and cannot separate them: the collector's late start, a feed disagreement inside the minutes both sources saw, and the window END, since the true window ran to 09:30 while the morning's stops at the 08:45 cutoff. The pm_*_collector_window columns exist to pull them apart. Full against collector window is what the window bounds cost; collector window against the live value is the feed disagreement on identical minutes, and only the second is a statement about the collector.

Since 2026-08-29 the same night takes the measurement from Alpaca's full SIP tape as well, which is a different vendor and a complete one. That pass writes pm_volume_true and, new that day, entry_ref_true and stop_ref_true: the reference levels every excursion is measured from, taken off the whole tape rather than off a socket sample. It then asks whether that level was a price anyone could have transacted at. Both are the section below.

  2.   3. One picks row, filled in four passes, and a ledger row beside it

The clearest way to see the flow is to follow a single database row. The scan creates it in the morning, the backfill and the truth pass widen it the same night, and the outcomes pass returns one and five trading sessions later to record what the market did next. Those outcome rows are the project's long game: the CRITERIA.md thresholds are unvalidated seed values until enough filled rows say otherwise. And the sample unit for that judgment is the session, not the row: twelve candidates from one morning share the same market, the same macro print, and often the same sector move, so they are one observation, not twelve. Any later analysis aggregates to one figure per session before comparing conviction buckets, and both fill_outcomes.py and weekly_page.py print both counts to keep that distinction visible.

The one that matters most: the outcome columns describe the wrong day

Writing the paper rule forced the question of which session it trades, and the answer exposed an off by one that had been in the record from the start.

The scan runs at 08:45 on the pick's own date and the report is about the open ninety minutes later. But next_day_open, next_day_high, next_day_low, next_day_close, pm_high_broke_next_day, mfe_pct, mae_pct and the two new _true excursions all describe the session AFTER that one. The session the report was actually about is measured by none of them.

AXTI, picked 2026-08-27, entry_ref 70.94
its own session: opened 70.30, reached 70.85 → a miss by 0.13%
next_day_high: 65.4155, from 2026-08-28 → mfe_pct reads -7.79

Nothing in the outcome columns was changed. Repointing them rewrites the meaning of every row already in the table and destroys the comparison. The ledger fetches its own bars for its own session instead, and the mismatch is written into CRITERIA [Paper], into paper_ledger's docstring, and into the ledger's own summary line, which states on every printing that the bound beside the booked P&L comes from a different day.

The rule, in full, because a rule that lives in code is one nobody can argue with

universeevery live picks row. Not only the eligible ones: day_eligible and swing_eligible travel onto the ledger row as GROUPING columns instead, so a later pass can ask whether the screen's verdict separated outcomes at all
skipany row whose fill_plausible is not 'plausible'. Written with its reason, never dropped
triggerthe first minute of the regular session whose high reaches entry_ref_true
entry pricemax(entry_ref_true, that minute's open). A session that gaps straight through the resting order fills at the open, which is the common case for a gap screen. HUT on 2026-08-27 opened 89.46 against an 88.90 trigger; booking the level would have turned a 3.00 percent loss into 2.38
stopstop_ref_true, the measured premarket low
same minutea minute that both triggers and reaches the stop is booked STOPPED. One bar carries no sequence, so the losing reading is taken
exitthe close of the session, if the stop was never reached. No target, deliberately: a target makes this a family with a parameter to fit, and holding to the close has nothing to tune
never firedno trade, and a NULL P&L. Zero would read as a flat trade, and 28 of the first 66 never fired
size10,000 dollars, whole shares, floor divided. Fixed notional because this table holds prices from 5.64 to 1,585

Two definitions of fill plausibility were built and thrown away first, both caught by the calibration rather than by review, and both are in DECISIONS.md. Counting a minute by its own volume weighted price is the obvious rule and measures the wrong thing: entry_ref is a session HIGH, an extreme no whole minute averages near, so BABA's 2,986,339 premarket shares over 268 minutes scored a band volume of zero. It ranked the most liquid names in the table as the least fillable. Requiring a minute COUNT as well fails the other way: MSTR traded 49,768 shares inside the band in a single minute, 1.4 million dollars, and calling that a print because it lasted one bar measures duration rather than liquidity. The verdict rests on notional alone and the minute count is recorded because it says how loose the volume bound is.

The band volume is an upper bound and is called one everywhere. A one minute bar carries o, h, l, c and v and no distribution, so a minute running from below up into the band counts whole. Correcting it needs trade level data this plan does not buy, so it is stated as a bound, the way premarket RVOL already is.

The fill band floor is now derived rather than asserted. [Truth] min_fill_band_notional was set at 250,000 as a placeholder before any rule named a position size. [Paper] then named 10,000 dollars at a 4 percent participation cap, and 10,000 / 0.04 is exactly 250,000. One shipped value, in [Truth], with the coupling machine checked, the same shape as the analyst timeout and the watchdog's stale window.

Added 2026-08-29, after the first ledger result

What v1 actually proved, and the two things built on it

Rule v1 lost 3,487.81 dollars over 16 trades and the obvious reading is that the screen does not work. The diagnostic says otherwise, and it says something more useful.

Every one of the 16 was in profit at some point while held

median best price reached while held +1.84% median given back by the hold-to-close -3.91 points losers that had been up more than 1% 6 of 11 losers never up more than 0.5% 2 of 11

NSSC was up 10.81 percent and closed -18.22. WOLF was up 6.36 and closed -7.72. PLAB was up 1.15 and closed -19.18. The trigger is not producing random entries.

But in units of the risk taken it is weak, and that is the finding. The median trade reached 0.46 of the risk it was carrying and only 4 of 16 ever reached 1R. A trade that risks 5.89 percent to reach 0.46 of that cannot work at any hit rate, and the two largest losses were the two widest stops.

The stop was never designed to be a stop. CRITERIA [Picks] says of entry_ref and stop_ref: "references for outcome measurement in later nightly jobs, never advice". The premarket low was chosen because it is a traded extreme that keeps excursion math interpretable. v1 borrowed it as a trade level because it was the level that existed, and on a gapper the premarket range is routinely 20 percent.

Rule v2 changes exactly one thing

The position size, from a fixed notional to a fixed dollar risk. Entry, stop, exit, tie break and skip rule are byte for byte v1's, so the two book the same trades and any difference is attributable to the sizing alone.

Two other candidates were refused and the refusals are the useful part. An exit rule, a target or a trail, would capture some of that 3.91 point give-back and is a free parameter that 16 trades will fit any value of. A different stop reference changes which trades exist, so the versions would stop booking the same trades and nothing could be attributed.

The risk budget holds TOTAL risk constant. v1's 16 trades carried 12,354 dollars of risk between them, a mean of 772; risk_notional is 750. The versions put the same money at risk and differ only in how it is spread. A smaller number would have manufactured a better v2.

v1, notional16 trades, total -3,487.81, 158,512 deployed, 12,354 at risk, worst single trade -1,912.54. Risk carried per trade ran 253 to 2,141: an eight fold spread across trades the rule treats as equals
v2, riskthe same 16 trades, total -2,713.74, 229,430 deployed, 11,814 at risk, worst single trade -748.90. Risk carried per trade 667 to 750, the spread being the whole-share rounding and the position cap
what did not changeevery per trade PERCENT return, identically. Median -1.38 percent and win rate 5 of 16 under both. Sizing cannot change what a trade did; it changes the portfolio's shape. CRITERIA [Paper] pre-registers the verdict at 200 trades across 60 sessions and 16 is not it

And the morning gets the fill warning

A third of what the report publishes has a headline level no market stands behind, and until now the reader found that out three weeks later in a database. Alpaca refuses a session that is still running, so the morning cannot run the real check; it computes its own band from the collector's sample instead, at 08:45, and prints it in Skips and traps.

It is a warning and never an approval, and the asymmetry is the design. Measured over all 66 rows:

morning: thincaught 6 of the 10 the night calls untradeable, and flagged 6 of the 44 that were fine
morning: not flagged38 the night agreed with, and 4 it did not. Four in ten untradeable levels get past this test, which is why its silence means nothing
morning: unknown12, the session with no collector coverage. Never "not flagged", which a reader would take for a clean bill

It cannot be calibrated better and the reason is structural: the morning centres its band on pm_high and the night on entry_ref_true, which differ by a median 1.19 percent and by up to 20.9, so on the names that matter most the two bands do not overlap. The socket's share of the band ran 0.017 to 1.158 of the night's figure across the same rows. A floor sweep from 5,000 to 100,000 dollars trades one error for the other at every step and never removes either.

It keeps its own vocabulary, thin and not flagged, never the night's plausible: a reader seeing that word at 08:45 would take it for the verdict that cannot exist until the session is over. It reads its band width from [Truth] rather than restating it, so the two bands cannot drift apart. And REPORT_TEMPLATE.md now tells the model, in the section itself, never to write that a level is tradeable.

Degradation, job by job

When something fails

The chain stops at the first failure, evidence gaps degrade to nulls with written reasons, and staleness is refused loudly in two places. Any job reading universe.json exits and names the file's age once it passes ten days. And scan.py calls vintage.py after pricing and before scoring, so a packet whose price timestamps, prior close dating, or high against close consistency say it is not this morning's data is refused outright. That second gate has no degrade path, because a stale price is not thin evidence a report can hedge around, it is a wrong number wearing the costume of a right one. vintage.py rewrites data/UNVERIFIED with every failing row named, nothing is scored, no packet is written, and scan exits non-zero, which stops the chain before the analyst. While the data/UNVERIFIED gate marker exists, no email goes out in any of these rows regardless of what else succeeded.

a market holiday arrives Every weekday job runs market_today.py first. On a weekend or an official exchange holiday (cached from EODHD exchange-details, refreshed by the nightly so the 08:45 window never fetches it) the job logs one line and exits cleanly: no watchlist from stale quotes, no empty collection, no report about a session that does not exist. If the calendar is unreachable and no cache exists, the guard assumes the market is open, because a false closed silently loses a real morning. That trade is only safe while "no cache" is rare, and until 2026-08-20 it was not: the nightly refresh deleted the cache before fetching, so one vendor outage at 22:15 left no holiday list at all and the next morning ran on the assumption that the market was open. The refresh now overwrites only once the vendor has answered.
universe.py fails Sunday The week runs on the last good universe.json until it turns ten days old, then discover and scan refuse to run, printing the age and the limit. No packet, no report, no email past that point.
discover.py fails at 07:15 No fresh watchlist and no warmed baselines. The collector exits naming the missing file (or runs on yesterday's list if one is on disk). Scan no longer rebuilds membership and makes no bulk call of its own: it reads the subscribed rows of watchlist.json, so a missing watchlist leaves it with no candidates at all and gaps_to_fill says exactly that. A watchlist that is present and subscribes nobody says so separately, because an empty pool and an absent file are different failures and only the second used to be named. A stale watchlist still on disk gives it yesterday's pool, which is also what the collector subscribed to, so the morning runs on the wrong names rather than on none. Either way the report goes out with the holes named in the disclaimer. That last sentence was false until 2026-08-20: every per candidate stage sits inside one branch of scan.py, one of its outputs was read outside that branch, and an empty pool ended the scan with an UnboundLocalError instead of a zero candidate packet, which stopped the chain and cost the whole morning. Fixed, and both routes into it are now claims in the suite.
collector never connects No minute bars, and price comes from the collector and nowhere else, so no candidate has a premarket price. Every subscribed name leaves the candidate list for the packet's dropped_no_coverage with its reason instead of being published at a stale price, so the packet carries zero candidates and gaps_to_fill names the missing collector file and every dropped name. The report still goes out: the market snapshot falls back to the end of day feed, each row labelled source eod and prior_session_only, the candidate tables are empty, and the disclaimer carries the reason.
scan.py fails at 08:45 No packet, so the chain stops before the model. No report, no email; the morning log ends at the scan step's exit code.
analyst.py fails or times out Two attempts at 537 seconds each, set at three times the slowest measured MORNING, 178.9 seconds on 2026-08-19, re-derived on 2026-08-20 off the five dry runs the schedule had overtaken. The morning that followed took 226.1 seconds, so the standing headroom is 2.4 times and the same rule is due to be applied again. Then the deterministic fallback: report.md is rebuilt as plain tables straight from packet.json, the disclaimer carries the failure reason, analyst_usage.json records status timeout or failed. Render and deliver carry on, the email still goes.
deliver.py fails The report is on disk in runs/<date>/ and the log holds Resend's answer. No email that morning and no morning archive rebuild, but the evening rebuild still catches the day; nothing else retries until the next scheduled morning.
build_archive.py fails site/PremarketDesk.html keeps its previous build. Every other artifact of the day is already on disk, and the next rebuild, evening or morning, replaces the file whole because every build is a full rebuild.
nightly jobs fail The true premarket columns and outcomes stay null. Both jobs are idempotent and fill only what is missing, so the next night's run picks up the backlog on its own. The morning report is no longer unaffected, and that is deliberate: every scheduled step records its outcome to data/job-status.jsonl as it exits, and the next morning's disclaimer names any step that has not succeeded inside the window CRITERIA.md gives it. A nightly failing quietly is precisely what went unnoticed for a week before that record existed.

Load bearing rules

Five decisions that shape everything above

EODHD is the only market data source in the pipeline.
REST for quotes, history, news, calendars; the websocket for live trades. No second vendor, ever, in anything that reaches a report. The exception is named rather than hidden: the research package carries Alpaca probes, written up in doc/ALPACA_PROBE.md, which exist to price an alternative feed against this one. Nothing in selection, collect, morning or night imports them, and no number they produce reaches picks or a packet.
Every threshold lives in doc/CRITERIA.md.
No Python file contains a screening number. criteria.py is a strict reader that raises on anything missing, so a threshold cannot quietly exist in two places.
Python decides, the model narrates.
Membership, eligibility, scores, and conviction are computed deterministically before analyst.py pipes the packet to the claude CLI. A containment check fails the run if the report mentions a ticker the packet does not carry.
Missing evidence is null, with a written reason.
A premarket RVOL that cannot be computed honestly is null plus a reason string, never a substituted guess. The report's disclaimer line names every such candidate.
Email is gated until a human verifies a real morning.
While data/UNVERIFIED exists, deliver.py refuses to send no matter what keys are configured. Only a human deletes that file.
